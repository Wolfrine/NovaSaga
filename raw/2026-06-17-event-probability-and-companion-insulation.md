# Raw Note - 2026-06-17 - Event Probability and Companion Insulation

## Source

User direction and Codex discussion on treating NovaSaga as an occurring event rather than a story premise.

## Context

The user explicitly clarified that NovaSaga should not be reasoned about first as "a story to tell." The Event should be treated as something already happening now, with likely consequences estimated realistically from physical, social, and behavioral probabilities.

This note is raw evidence for later curation. It should not be promoted into active wiki as a plot memo without rewriting into lore-record language.

## Core framing

The Event should be considered as a live systems failure rather than a narrative device.

A completely literal end of electricity everywhere would break biology, weather, chemistry, nerves, muscles, and the brain. The more realistic working model is narrower:

> The Event disrupts engineered electrical conduction, especially metallic circuits, power grids, electronics, generators, batteries, and long-distance transmission, while biological electrochemistry continues to function.

This makes the world survivable but civilization-breaking.

## Likely visible-world outcomes

- Most people first interpret the Event as technological collapse, cyberwar, solar storm, electromagnetic attack, divine punishment, government failure, or a combination of these.
- Cities become unstable quickly because water pumps, refrigeration, hospitals, payment systems, elevators, traffic systems, fuel pumps, and communications fail.
- Local trust becomes more important than national authority within days. Neighborhoods, temples, schools, farms, clinics, and local leaders become survival nodes.
- Rumor becomes one of the strongest forces shaping behavior. Fear spreads faster than verified information.
- Governments and militaries do not vanish immediately, but their effective reach shrinks toward food, water, ports, armories, and administrative centers.
- Rural and semi-rural communities probably adapt better than dense urban populations, assuming water and food access remain possible.
- Violence rises, but not uniformly. Some places organize mutual aid before they collapse into predation.

## Likely hidden-world outcomes

- Most weakened barriers, breaches, or hidden-realm contacts are not understood clearly at first. They are likely misread as hallucination, disaster trauma, cult rumor, animal attack, local supernatural legend, or strange weather.
- Luminaries likely avoid open revelation. Public proof would create panic, worship, exploitation, and weaponized belief.
- Accidental contact is more likely near old boundary places: rivers, forests, caves, ruins, temples, mountains, storm zones, abandoned sites, and emotionally charged landscapes.
- Dark forces spread first through already unstable places: mobs, prisons, hospitals, refugee shelters, battle zones, corrupt leadership circles, grief-heavy homes, and communities already poisoned by fear or despair.
- Dark beings may be mistaken for animals, infected humans, criminals, hallucinations, or local monsters before anyone understands them as part of a wider unseen order.

## Less likely early outcomes

- A clean, visible global Luminary-versus-dark-forces war is unlikely at first. Communication collapse and public disbelief would make coordination difficult.
- Public acceptance of Luminaries is unlikely. Even direct witnesses would disagree about what they saw.
- Visible use of portals or interplanetary allies is unlikely early. Such revelation would destabilize the already collapsing world too quickly.
- Newly contacted ordinary youths would probably not be trusted immediately with major power. They would be watched, tested, and exposed to knowledge gradually.

## Key principle

The Event does not reveal the hidden world clearly. It makes reality ambiguous.

People see enough to be changed, but not enough to agree. Hidden truth must compete with immediate survival: dead phones, spoiled food, failed water systems, panic, family danger, and neighbors deciding whether to cooperate or exploit one another.

## Companion insulation instinct

The user described a strong instinct that the first companion group should not experience the gradual public suffering caused by electricity dissipation in real time.

Instead, the boys should be kept away from ordinary society during the first phase. They get into trouble, encounter astonishing and unbelievable hidden phenomena, and only afterward return to the visible world and discover the scale of human suffering.

The user feels this narration is important, though the full reasons remain open.

## Possible reasons this instinct may be structurally important

- It preserves shock. The boys return with one impossible experience in mind, only to find a second impossibility in ordinary life.
- It prevents the early account from becoming disaster reportage before the hidden order has marked them.
- It lets the boys encounter the hidden world while still mentally belonging to the old normal world.
- It makes their later service more meaningful because they choose to help after disorientation, not because they have already adapted step by step.
- It creates an information gap between the companions and everyone else. The public has lived the collapse hour by hour; the companions missed those hours and must catch up morally, practically, and emotionally.
- It makes their witness unstable. They return from something no one will believe into a world where everyone already has urgent suffering of their own.
- It protects ambiguity. The boys cannot easily separate the hidden-realm incident from the global collapse, but they also cannot prove the connection.
- It delays exposition. The early record can remain sensory and immediate: river, storm, barrier failure, hostile forms, rescue, confusion, return.
- It creates guilt. The companions may feel they were absent while family, neighbors, and ordinary people suffered.
- It gives the Luminary mentor a clearer reason to watch them: not only because they saw hidden things, but because their conduct after returning reveals whether the encounter changed them toward service or fear.
